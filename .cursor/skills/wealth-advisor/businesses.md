# Business Playbooks

All figures are rough ranges for planning. Verify them with local market data, real financial statements, and an accountant before committing money.

## Valuation basics

- **SDE (seller's discretionary earnings)**: profit plus the owner's salary and personal expenses run through the business
- **Value**: small service businesses usually sell for about 2-4x SDE
- **Payback period**: total cash invested divided by yearly owner earnings
- Prefer buying a business with 3+ years of verified profit over starting from zero

## Software development or agency

| Item | Range |
|------|-------|
| Startup capital | $2-20k |
| Net margin | 15-30% |
| Payback period | 3-12 months |
| Sale multiple | 2-4x SDE, higher for recurring revenue |

Main risks:
- Dependence on one or two clients
- The owner being the only person who can deliver
- Fixed-price projects that run over budget

Key metrics:
- Billable utilization (target 70-80%)
- Effective hourly rate
- Share of revenue from the largest client (keep under 30%)
- Monthly recurring revenue from retainers or SaaS

First 90 days:
1. Pick a niche (industry and tech stack) and write a one-page offer
2. Land 2-3 paying clients through your network, Upwork, Toptal, or LinkedIn
3. Set rates at least 2x your salary-equivalent hourly rate
4. Use contracts with milestones and upfront deposits
5. Hire or subcontract the first developer once utilization stays above 80%

## Car repair station

| Item | Range |
|------|-------|
| Startup capital (new) | $100-500k |
| Acquisition of an existing shop | 2-3x SDE, often with an SBA loan or seller financing |
| Net margin | 10-20% |
| Payback period | 2-5 years |

Main risks:
- Finding and keeping qualified mechanics
- Lease terms and location
- Equipment costs
- Liability for mistakes
- The shift to electric vehicles

Key metrics:
- Revenue per bay per day
- Car count per week
- Average repair order value
- Technician efficiency (billed hours compared with paid hours)
- Parts margin and comeback rate

First 90 days:
1. Partner with an experienced mechanic or buy a shop with staff who are staying
2. Review 3 years of financials, the lease, equipment condition, and customer reviews
3. Set up a shop management system (for example Tekmetric or Shopmonkey) to track repair orders
4. Add fleet and B2B contracts for stable volume
5. Track car count and average repair order value every week

## Gym

| Item | Range |
|------|-------|
| Startup capital (boutique studio) | $50-300k |
| Startup capital (large gym) | $500k+ |
| Net margin | 10-20% |
| Payback period | 2-5 years |

Main risks:
- Membership churn
- Seasonality, with signups peaking in January
- Long, expensive leases
- Competition from low-cost chains
- Equipment financing

Key metrics:
- Active members
- Monthly churn (target under 5%)
- Average revenue per member
- Customer acquisition cost
- Revenue from personal training and add-ons

First 90 days:
1. Choose a niche (strength, CrossFit-style, martial arts, women-only, rehab) instead of competing with low-cost chains
2. Pre-sell memberships before signing the lease
3. Negotiate the lease with a rent-free fit-out period and an exit clause
4. Run retention programs from day one (onboarding sessions, challenges, community events)
5. Track churn and acquisition cost monthly

## Decision checklist before any business

- [ ] Foundation complete (emergency fund, no high-interest debt)
- [ ] Capital at risk is money that can be lost without breaking the plan
- [ ] At least 3 years of verified financials reviewed (for acquisitions)
- [ ] Payback period under 4 years in the base case
- [ ] Clear plan for who runs daily operations
- [ ] Lawyer reviewed the lease and contracts; accountant reviewed the structure and taxes
