# $1M in 7 Years Roadmap

## Math

```
r = (1 + annual_return)^(1/12) - 1
FV of monthly contribution C over n months = C * ((1 + r)^n - 1) / r
```

For n = 84 months the multiplier on each $1 invested monthly is:

| Annual return | Multiplier | Monthly amount for $1M |
|---------------|------------|------------------------|
| 7% | ~107 | ~$9,330 |
| 10% | ~119 | ~$8,400 |
| 15% | ~142 | ~$7,060 |

Portfolio value after 7 years at 10% for a fixed monthly contribution:

| Monthly contribution | Value after 7 years |
|----------------------|---------------------|
| $1,000 | ~$119k |
| $2,000 | ~$238k |
| $3,000 | ~$357k |
| $5,000 | ~$595k |
| $8,400 | ~$1,000k |

Conclusion: with a $3-5k salary the plan needs both rising contributions and business equity.

## Phase 1: Years 1-2, foundation and income growth

- Build the emergency fund and clear high-interest debt
- Open IBKR (or a local low-cost broker) and automate a monthly ETF purchase
- Contribute $1,500/month in year 1 and $2,500/month in year 2
- Move into a higher-paying IT role and start freelancing 5-15 hours per week
- Target income: $6-8k/month by the end of year 2

## Phase 2: Years 2-4, side business and scaling income

- Turn freelancing into a small software agency, or launch a niche SaaS product
- Contribute $4,000/month in year 3 and $5,000/month in year 4
- Target total income: $10k+/month by the end of year 4
- Start studying car repair stations or gyms for sale in the area: request financials, learn valuation multiples, and talk to owners

## Phase 3: Years 4-7, profitable business and equity

- Grow the business to $150-250k of yearly owner earnings (SDE)
- Contribute $6,000/month in years 5-7 and reinvest excess business profit
- Optionally buy an existing profitable car repair station or gym using an SBA loan or seller financing, with a manager in place so it does not consume all the owner's time
- Keep the brokerage portfolio separate from business risk; never sell index ETFs to cover business losses without a written plan

## What does not work

- Day trading, signal groups, and "prop firm" challenges: most participants lose money
- Margin, leveraged ETFs, and short options to "speed up" growth
- Putting most of the net worth into one stock or one crypto asset
- Starting a capital-heavy business (new gym, new car repair station) with borrowed money and no operating experience
